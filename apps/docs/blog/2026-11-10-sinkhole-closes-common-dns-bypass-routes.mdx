Sinkhole for pfSense 1.0, released on November 10, 2026, includes a Bypass tab that keeps devices on the interfaces you choose from going around the firewall's DNS, the gap that lets devices sidestep most DNS blocking.

{/* truncate */}

## Why It Matters

DNS blocking only works while devices ask the firewall. A device with a hardcoded resolver, DNS over HTTPS, Tor, or a VPN can skip the block lists entirely.

Closing those paths used to mean hand-made NAT redirect rules and firewall rules per interface. The Bypass tab turns each protection into one setting, applied to the interfaces you select.

## What's on the Bypass Tab

Every protection is off by default, and no interface is selected until you choose one.

### Plain DNS

- **Redirect** — Sends ordinary DNS on port 53 to the firewall while keeping each device's source address, so network and device lists still apply.

### Encrypted DNS

- **TLS and QUIC** — Blocks port 853 to every destination except the firewall.
- **Providers** — Blocks the names and addresses on encrypted-DNS provider lists you add.
- **Firefox** — Turns off Firefox's automatic DNS over HTTPS selection.
- **Private Relay** — Tells compatible Apple devices that iCloud Private Relay isn't available on this network.
- **Discovery** — Answers encrypted-DNS discovery queries, or advertises the firewall's own DNS over TLS.

### Tunnels

- **Tor** — Blocks listed Tor relays.
- **VPN** — Blocks listed VPN providers and the standard VPN ports: TCP and UDP 1194, UDP 51820, UDP 500 and 4500, and ESP.

Bypass Lists are added by URL or from the Bypass catalog, update on the list schedule, and keep their last good copy when a download fails.

## What It Doesn't Promise

These controls close the common routes, not every route. A custom encrypted tunnel can still get past DNS-based policy, and the docs say so plainly.

## Get Started

Open **Firewall → Sinkhole → Bypass**, select the client interfaces, and turn on the protections you want. [Bypass Protection](/docs/configure/bypass-protection) covers each setting.

Sinkhole for pfSense brings scoped DNS, IP, and country blocking to pfSense, with every list saved on the firewall.
